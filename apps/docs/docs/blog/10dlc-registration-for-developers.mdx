Most 10DLC guides are written for whoever fills in the registration form. They cover opt-in pages, privacy policies, and campaign descriptions. If you write the code that sends the texts, you have a different problem: the first time you send from an unregistered US number, the API accepts the message, your code logs a success, and the text never arrives.

This post covers the developer side: what 10DLC registration is, the steps in order, what each part attaches to, how a missing registration shows up in your code, and how to catch it. The examples use SMS SDK, the open-source TypeScript library this site documents. It calls your own Twilio, Telnyx, Plivo, Vonage, Bandwidth, or Sinch account. It isn't a carrier or an SMS provider, and it doesn't register anything for you. Registration rules change and differ by provider and use case, so treat this as plumbing, not legal advice.

## The short answer

10DLC means a standard 10-digit US phone number used for application-to-person (A2P) messages. US carriers require those numbers to be registered before they carry your traffic:

1. A **brand** says who is sending: your business, or you as a sole proprietor.
2. A **campaign** says what you send and how people opt in, opt out, and get help.
3. Each **number** you send from is attached to an approved campaign, usually through a group your provider manages (a Twilio Messaging Service, a Telnyx messaging profile).

Send from a number that isn't attached and carriers block the message. Your provider usually accepts the API request first. The failure comes later on the status webhook: error **30034** on Twilio, **40010** on Telnyx. Your code only sees it if it reads the message's final status, from the status webhook or by looking the message up later.

## How to register for A2P 10DLC, step by step

You don't register with The Campaign Registry yourself. Brands can't sign up there directly. Your SMS provider acts as the campaign service provider and submits for you. Direct registration on Twilio and Telnyx follows this order:

1. **Use a paid account and a US 10DLC number.** Twilio trial accounts can't register for A2P 10DLC.
2. **On Twilio, create the business profile.** It's a Trust Hub customer profile (a Primary or Secondary Compliance Profile) with your legal name, address, and tax ID, such as a US EIN. Sole proprietors in the US or Canada without a tax ID use their personal details. Telnyx collects these details when you create the brand.
3. **Register the brand.** Your provider sends it to The Campaign Registry. Twilio texts sole proprietors a one-time code at the mobile number they gave, and it has to be answered within 24 hours. Telnyx lists standard third-party brand vetting at 1 to 7 business days.
4. **Register the campaign.** Describe the use case in full sentences, give two sample messages that name your business and say how to opt out ("Reply STOP to opt out"), and describe exactly how people opt in. Twilio says most application failures happen during campaign registration, and that reviews for standard use cases take up to 5 business days. For a website opt-in, Twilio's reviewers expect the signup page URL, a privacy policy, and terms of service, and a gated page or paper form needs a hosted screenshot or document ([error 30896](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30896)).
5. **Attach your sending numbers.** On Twilio, add each number to the sender pool of the Messaging Service linked to the approved campaign. A Sole Proprietor campaign takes only one number. On Telnyx, [assign the number](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/phone-number-assignment) to a messaging profile, then to the campaign. Each number belongs to one campaign at a time.
6. **Wait for the number to finish registering, then test.** Twilio returns error [30035](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30035) while a number is still pending registration, which is different from 30034. Wait until your provider shows the number as `REGISTERED` (Twilio) or `ASSIGNED` ([Telnyx](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/6325734-how-to-assign-a-number-to-a-campaign)), then send to a test recipient who opted in and check that the status webhook reports delivered. An API request can be accepted even when the sending number is unregistered. It then fails with 30034 or 40010, as the sections below show.

If your fallback provider also sends from US 10DLC numbers, check its brand, campaign, and number assignment too. A Twilio registration doesn't carry over to Telnyx on its own.

Sources: Twilio's [A2P 10DLC registration quickstart](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc/quickstart) and [error 30034](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30034) and [30035](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30035), Telnyx's [10DLC quickstart](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/quickstart) and [number assignment guide](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/phone-number-assignment), and [The Campaign Registry](https://www.campaignregistry.com/), checked 2026-11-27.

## Brands, campaigns, and numbers

Registration happens in your provider's console or API, and the provider passes it on to The Campaign Registry, the industry database carriers check. The two supported providers describe it this way:

| | Twilio | Telnyx |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand | Sole Proprietor (one campaign, no tax ID, US and Canada only), Low-Volume Standard, or Standard (normally up to five campaigns each, tax ID required) | The business sending the messages; vetting gives a trust score from 0 to 100 that sets your throughput |
| Campaign | Your use case, message samples, and how people opt in, opt out, and get help. Campaigns get a manual review. | One per distinct kind of messaging. Created at once, then pending carrier approval. |
| Numbers | In the sender pool of the Messaging Service linked to the approved campaign | Assigned to a messaging profile first, then to the campaign |
| Register from code | The API, for ISVs registering their customers | `POST /v2/10dlc/brand`, `/v2/10dlc/campaignBuilder`, `/v2/10dlc/phoneNumberCampaign` |
| Unregistered send fails with | 30034, "Message from an Unregistered Number" | 40010, "Unregistered 10DLC Message" |

Sources: Twilio's [A2P 10DLC overview](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc) and [error 30034](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30034), Telnyx's [10DLC quickstart](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/quickstart) and [messaging error codes](https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/6505121-telnyx-messaging-error-codes), checked 2026-11-03. Brand vetting and campaign review can each take days, so leave time before a launch. Plivo, Vonage, Bandwidth, and Sinch have their own steps; [Registration and consent](/providers/registration-and-consent) links their docs.

Three details catch developers more than the forms do:

- **Registration doesn't carry over automatically.** A campaign registered through Twilio doesn't cover your Telnyx numbers by itself. If you configure a fallback provider, its 10DLC numbers need a brand, campaign, and assignment there too, or fallback sends from them fail the same way.
- **The number has to be in the right group.** Twilio's 30034 page lists a number missing from the Messaging Service's sender pool as a cause, even when the campaign is approved. Buying a new number and sending from it directly is the classic way to hit this.
- **Toll-free numbers and short codes are not 10DLC.** They have their own verification or application process. Twilio suggests them, or its Verify product, as alternatives when a number can't be registered.

## How a missing registration shows up in your code

Here is the sequence on Twilio when the sender isn't registered:

1. `send()` resolves. Twilio accepted the request and returned a message SID, so SMS SDK reports a handoff, not a delivery.
2. The carrier route refuses the message.
3. Twilio calls your status webhook with `MessageStatus=failed` or `undelivered` and `ErrorCode=30034`.

SMS SDK's webhook parser turns that into a `message.undelivered` event with `errorCode: "30034"`. On Telnyx, a `message.finalized` webhook with a failed status and error 40010 becomes the same event type with `errorCode: "40010"`. Without a status webhook or a later message lookup, your app logs the handoff and never sees the delivery failure.

This is also why [provider fallback](/sending/fallback-and-retries) can't rescue an unregistered number. SMS SDK falls back only when a provider refuses a send at send time with a code the adapter knows, such as a `sender` or `account` rejection. By the time 30034 arrives, the send has already been handed off. Retrying automatically on another provider at that point is how people get the same text twice. The fix is to register the number, not to retry.

Telnyx's own docs have not been consistent here: its [10DLC troubleshooting guide](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/10dlc/troubleshooting) listed 40300 for an unregistered number when we checked on 2026-11-03, while its [messaging error reference](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/messages/error-codes) defines 40300 as a block after a STOP reply and says not to retry. SMS SDK treats 40300 as an opt-out refusal, so it never falls back on it. If you see 40300 from a number nobody opted out of, check the number's campaign.

### Which Twilio errors point to 10DLC registration?

Not every failed status means the number is unregistered. On Twilio:

| Code | What Twilio says it means | A 10DLC problem? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30034 | Message from an unregistered number: the 10DLC number isn't attached to an approved campaign | Yes |
| 30007 | Message filtered: blocked by Twilio or the carrier, for example as spam | Not by itself. Check the content and how people opted in. |
| 30003 | Unreachable destination handset: the phone is off, has no signal, or can't receive SMS | No |

Twilio's [30003 page](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30003) adds that repeated 30003 failures from a long code can also point to carrier filtering. [30007](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30007) is the code to watch for content and consent problems. Both checked 2026-11-27. The module below flags only 30034 and 40010, so a 30003 or 30007 never pages anyone about registration. [SMS delivery reports for developers](/blog/sms-delivery-reports) covers the other status codes.

## Catch it from the status webhook

This module picks registration failures out of verified events. It ignores everything else, so you can call it on every webhook:

```ts registration-alerts.ts
import type { SmsEvent } from "@opencoredev/sms-sdk/webhooks";

// Error codes each provider documents for a send from an unregistered 10DLC number.
const UNREGISTERED_CODES: Partial<Record<SmsEvent["provider"], readonly string[]>> = {
  twilio: ["30034"],
  telnyx: ["40010"],
};

export type RegistrationFailure = {
  readonly provider: SmsEvent["provider"];
  readonly providerId: string;
  readonly errorCode: string;
  readonly from: string | undefined;
};

/** Returns the failure when a status event says the sending number isn't registered. */
export function registrationFailure(event: SmsEvent): RegistrationFailure | undefined {
  if (event.type !== "message.undelivered" && event.type !== "message.filtered") return undefined;
  if (event.errorCode === undefined) return undefined;
  if (!(UNREGISTERED_CODES[event.provider] ?? []).includes(event.errorCode)) return undefined;
  return { provider: event.provider, providerId: event.providerId, errorCode: event.errorCode, from: event.from };
}
```

Then call it from the route that receives status callbacks. This one takes Twilio. A Telnyx route is the same with `provider: "telnyx"` and its `publicKey`:

```ts
import { parseSmsWebhook, WebhookPayloadError, WebhookSignatureError } from "@opencoredev/sms-sdk/webhooks";
import { db } from "./db";
import { alertOnCall } from "./notify";
import { registrationFailure } from "./registration-alerts";

export async function POST(request: Request): Promise<Response> {
  try {
    const event = await parseSmsWebhook({
      provider: "twilio",
      request,
      credentials: { authToken: process.env.TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN ?? "" },
      publicUrl: "https://example.com/api/webhooks/sms/status",
    });
    await db.transaction(async (tx) => {
      // Providers repeat callbacks. Handle each event once; a throw below rolls this back so a retry can alert.
      if (!(await tx.webhookEvents.insertIfNew(event.dedupeKey))) return;
      if ("providerStatus" in event) {
        await tx.messages.updateStatus(event.providerId, event.type, event.errorCode);
      }
      const failure = registrationFailure(event);
      if (failure !== undefined) {
        // Every send from this number fails until it's attached to a campaign. Page someone.
        await alertOnCall(`SMS sender ${failure.from ?? "unknown"} is not 10DLC registered (${failure.provider} ${failure.errorCode})`, failure);
      }
    });
    return new Response(null, { status: 200 });
  } catch (error) {
    if (error instanceof WebhookSignatureError) return new Response("Invalid signature", { status: 401 });
    if (error instanceof WebhookPayloadError) return new Response("Unexpected payload", { status: 400 });
    throw error;
  }
}
```

Providers repeat status callbacks, so the route records each event's `dedupeKey` and pages once per failed message. If the alert or the database write throws, the route answers 500 and rolls back. Twilio doesn't retry a 500 by default, though, so add connection overrides such as `#rc=3&rp=5xx` to the status callback URL you configure in Twilio, and keep `publicUrl` without the fragment. The [Twilio guide](/providers/twilio) covers this. One failure means every message from that number is failing, so an alert beats a log line. If you send one-time codes, also show the user a "didn't get a code?" path that doesn't depend on the same number. [SMS OTP API in TypeScript](/blog/sms-otp-api-typescript) covers resends.

## Test it without sending texts

The signed request builders in `@opencoredev/sms-sdk/testing` make webhook requests that pass the real signature checks, so you can feed your route the exact failure a carrier would produce:

```ts registration-alerts.test.ts
import { expect, test } from "bun:test";
import { generateTelnyxKeyPair, signedTelnyxRequest, signedTwilioRequest } from "@opencoredev/sms-sdk/testing";
import { parseSmsWebhook } from "@opencoredev/sms-sdk/webhooks";
import { registrationFailure } from "./registration-alerts";

const url = "https://example.com/api/webhooks/sms/status";
const authToken = "test-auth-token";

async function twilioStatus(status: string, errorCode?: string) {
  const params: Record<string, string> = {
    MessageSid: `SM${crypto.randomUUID().replaceAll("-", "")}`,
    MessageStatus: status,
    From: "+15550100002",
    To: "+14155550123",
  };
  if (errorCode !== undefined) params["ErrorCode"] = errorCode;
  const request = await signedTwilioRequest({ authToken, url, params });
  return parseSmsWebhook({ provider: "twilio", request, credentials: { authToken }, publicUrl: url });
}

test("Twilio 30034 is a registration failure", async () => {
  const failure = registrationFailure(await twilioStatus("failed", "30034"));
  expect(failure).toMatchObject({ provider: "twilio", errorCode: "30034", from: "+15550100002" });
});

test("a delivered message and other errors are not", async () => {
  expect(registrationFailure(await twilioStatus("delivered"))).toBeUndefined();
  expect(registrationFailure(await twilioStatus("undelivered", "30003"))).toBeUndefined();
});

test("Telnyx 40010 is a registration failure", async () => {
  const { privateKey, publicKey } = await generateTelnyxKeyPair();
  const body = {
    data: {
      id: `evt_${crypto.randomUUID()}`,
      event_type: "message.finalized",
      payload: {
        id: `msg_${crypto.randomUUID()}`,
        from: { phone_number: "+15550100002" },
        to: [{ phone_number: "+14155550123", status: "delivery_failed" }],
        errors: [{ code: "40010", title: "Unregistered 10DLC Message" }],
      },
    },
  };
  const request = await signedTelnyxRequest({ privateKey, url, body });
  const event = await parseSmsWebhook({ provider: "telnyx", request, credentials: { publicKey } });
  expect(registrationFailure(event)).toMatchObject({ provider: "telnyx", errorCode: "40010" });
});
```

## A checklist before you send to US numbers

- Check the campaign and number assignment for every US 10DLC sender, including fallback senders. Registration doesn't carry over automatically.
- Attach every sending number to the campaign: Twilio's Messaging Service sender pool, Telnyx's messaging profile plus campaign assignment.
- Send from the group (the Messaging Service) rather than a bare number where your provider supports it, so new numbers don't skip the campaign.
- Point status webhooks at your app and alert on 30034 and 40010.
- Run `npx @opencoredev/sms-sdk doctor --adapter <provider>`. It checks your configuration without sending, and it reports 10DLC registration as "not verified" because the dry run doesn't call your provider. Check campaign status and number assignment in your provider's console or API.
- Keep consent records and handle STOP and HELP. Carriers review these during campaign vetting. [SMS opt-out handling](/blog/sms-opt-out-handling) covers the code, and [STOP, HELP, and opt-outs](/receiving/opt-outs) lists the events SMS SDK gives you.

## FAQ

### Can I register for 10DLC on a Twilio trial account?

No. You have to upgrade to a paid Twilio account to register a 10DLC number. Short codes are paid-only too, and a toll-free number needs [toll-free verification](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/tutorials/how-to-use-your-free-trial-account) before it can text US numbers.

### Do I need 10DLC registration for one-time codes and notifications?

If you send them from a standard US 10-digit number, yes. Twilio says carriers treat every message sent from its numbers as A2P traffic, whatever the content. Toll-free numbers, short codes, and hosted verification products have their own processes instead.

### Does the API tell me the number isn't registered when I send?

Usually not. On Twilio the request is accepted and error 30034 arrives on the status callback. Telnyx lists 40010 among its delivery errors, reported after the send. Plan for the webhook either way.

### Does SMS SDK register numbers or check registration?

No. It sends through your provider account and reports what the provider says. Registration is between you and your provider. SMS SDK's `doctor` command lists it as "not verified" so it isn't forgotten.

### Will fallback to another provider fix a 30034?

No. The failure arrives after the handoff, and SMS SDK never resends a handed-off message on its own. A fallback 10DLC number would also need its own campaign assignment. Register the number and resend deliberately if the message still matters.
